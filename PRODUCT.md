# Product

## Register

product

## Users

**Students first.** Mostly BUE Faculty of Informatics & Computer Science students (Year 1–4, by specialization). They use the site during a live lab session on a laptop or phone, on shared campus Wi-Fi, and often follow a projector at the front of the room. They read a lab step by step, run React Native exercises in the browser, tick tasks, hand in files and check in for attendance. Many never create an account: they type their name and student ID in each lab.

**Staff second.** TAs run lab sessions (start a session, watch progress, allow a new device, take attendance). Doctors and module leaders build modules and labs in the visual builder, set assessment weights, invite their team and students, review project hand-ins and groups. One super admin manages roles, the catalogue and accounts.

## Product Purpose

BUENOVA is the faculty's interactive course platform. It started as the Mobile Development (25CSCI38H) course site and now hosts any module: labs with live, auto-checked exercises, session-gated progress and attendance, project hand-ins, and a builder for new modules.

Success: a student opens the right lab in seconds, always knows where they are and what to do next, and finishes the session with progress and attendance recorded. A TA can run a whole lab from a phone. A doctor can publish a new lab without a developer.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, technical, energetic. It feels like a modern developer tool (dark first, lime accent, mono details), but it is calm and easy to read during a 2-hour lab. Confident, plain copy that explains why, never jargon or hype.

## Anti-references

- Generic LMS (Moodle, Blackboard): cluttered menus, grey boxes, everything one click too deep.
- Childish gamified apps: badges, confetti, cartoon rewards that do not fit university students.
- Bare documentation sites: walls of text with no guidance, progress or structure.

## Design Principles

1. **The next step is always obvious.** Every screen answers "where am I, what do I do now, how do I go back" (one primary action, clear back/forward).
2. **Built for the lab room.** Phones, projectors, sunlight, slow Wi-Fi: content is readable and visible immediately, controls are easy to hit, nothing depends on perfect conditions.
3. **Show, then explain.** Live demos, previews and real examples beat paragraphs; explanations stay short and next to the thing they explain.
4. **Energy in the accent, calm in the content.** Lime, glow and motion mark what matters (progress, the current step, the primary action); reading surfaces stay quiet.
5. **Honest status.** Sessions, saves, uploads and permissions always say what happened and why something is unavailable.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 text contrast in both themes, full keyboard use with visible focus, screen-reader names and landmarks, 24×24px minimum targets (44px for primary actions on phones), 16px form fields on phones, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected everywhere, and no content hidden until scripts run.
